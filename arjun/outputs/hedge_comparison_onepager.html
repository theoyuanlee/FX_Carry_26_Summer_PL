01 What the notebook does

The book. Rank 27 currencies by forward-implied carry; long high-yielders, funded by shorting the low-yielders (JPY, CHF). Vol-targeted 10%, net of costs. A reconciliation gate reproduces the committed Sharpes exactly before any test runs.

Part A — the JPY hedge, three ways. The book is short JPY ~79% of days on purpose (its best G10 earner), so a plain static long-JPY overlay is just un-sizing a profitable leg — not a hedge. The notebook therefore tests the versions that could work: (A.1) static hedge-ratio scaling of the JPY leg, (A.2) cutting or flipping the funding legs only when a tradeable FX-vol trigger fires, and (A.3) a variance-minimising JY1 overlay on the whole book — full hedge-ratio sweep, real-time expanding & rolling-beta dynamic hedging, and stress-gated hybrids.

Part B — five futures classes, one yardstick. Every instrument (JY1, SF1, TU1, FV1, TY1, US1, SPXT/ES1, DX1, UX1/SPVXSTR) vs the book, with each hedged Sharpe decomposed into variance reduction vs the instrument's own return — so an instrument's bull market or a data artifact can't masquerade as hedging skill.

Data honesty. Real 2026-08 futures pulls (JY1, SF1, SPXT), gated to be static values; every generic futures series is cross-checked against a spot+forward replica to strip the roll-splice artifact (~200 bp/yr of phantom return in JY1/SF1; +46%/yr in VIX).

02 Key findings

  • Nothing investable beats de-risking. Judged against cutting exposure in stress (Sharpe 0.571), every honest futures overlay fails — most also land below the unhedged 0.466.
  • JPY is real insurance at full price. Best tail payoff (+32–34 bp/day) and ~10% vol reduction, but the carry premium eats it; max drawdown gets worse (2013–15 JPY collapse).
  • Dynamic hedging doesn't rescue it — the JPY beta never changes sign, so rolling re-estimation adds noise, not timing: dynamic 0.25–0.37 vs static 0.31–0.43.
  • The funding legs aren't the crash risk. Cutting JPY/CHF legs in stress hurts (0.119 → 0.068–0.091 on the G10 book); the stress factor is broad risk appetite, spread across all legs.
  • Artifact watch. The two rows that nominally clear the bar are both measurement artifacts: UX1 generic (phantom +46%/yr roll gain) and SF1 (0.576 → 0.469 on replica pricing).

Bottom line

No futures overlay — static or dynamic — makes this book better. Everything that pays in a carry crash is priced as insurance and charges for it on every calm day; everything free is too weak or blind to the book's EM-risk-appetite exposure. The cheapest crash protection is to condition the book itself: de-risk on the FX-vol trigger (Sharpe 0.466 → 0.571), or the stress-triggered EM-equity short — not an always-on futures hedge.
