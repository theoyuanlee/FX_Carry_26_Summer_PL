# Component verdicts — what was kept, what was dropped, and what was never tested

**The claim this package makes is that six workstreams were each tested on one common book and kept
or dropped against a bar written down before the run.** This is that claim, itemised. Sixteen
components, six workstreams, two adoptions — and everything else, at the same level of detail.

Generated by [`verdicts.py`](verdicts.py) into
[`evidence/component_verdicts.csv`](evidence/component_verdicts.csv), with every number read out of
a committed CSV rather than typed in. Run `python final/verdicts.py` to rebuild it.

**Why the rejections are here.** Nine attempts have failed to beat the simple book and not one
result anywhere in this project has a statistically significant net alpha — the largest |t| on any
rung of the integration ladder is 1.16. A verdict table that showed only the two adoptions would
imply a hit rate this work does not have. The nulls are the most defensible thing in it: each was
measured against a bar fixed in advance, on the same book, with the CSV still in the repo. They are
the reason the two adoptions are worth anything.

### The bars, fixed before any of it was measured

- **Slot rule (§19.4)** — a component earns a place in the combined book if, net of costs, it
  (i) improves MaxDD **or** CVaR₉₉ in **≥ 4 of the 6** pre-2026 stress windows, **and** (ii) costs
  less than 0.05 whole-sample net Sharpe, **and** (iii) survives leave-one-out.
- **Tail objective (§19.3)** — accept a de-risking rule if its net Sharpe cost is ≤ 0.02 **and** it
  buys ≥ 1.0 pp of MaxDD **or** ≥ 5% relative CVaR₉₉, demonstrated **per episode**.
- **The Sharpe bars (§17, §19)** — beat the baseline (net 0.4659) **and** the per-currency-RR book
  (0.4559), with Newey–West significance.

### Two caveats that travel with every reading of this table

1. **The adopted overlay is mostly de-risking, not selection.** Of the bad-skew filter's 7.3 pp
   drawdown improvement, **6.8 pp is holding less notional and 0.5 pp is the signal**, and the
   selection alpha is +1.25%/yr at **t = 0.92** — insignificant. What selection genuinely buys is
   skew, −0.63 → −0.31, which de-risking does not deliver at all. Without the gross-matched control
   (`evidence/p4_selection_vs_derisking.csv`, which reproduces the overlay's daily gross to 8.9e-16)
   this would have been the most misleading number in the project.
2. **Four of five components are re-priced, not rebuilt.** Base adoption reached **1 of 5** — only
   Dafu ported onto the shared base — so the rest were folded in from committed outputs under the
   §15 fallback. A re-price is *our* reading of a teammate's signal, not their specification of it.
   Every row carries its reconstruction method; that column is part of the deliverable.

## The one contested verdict: Dafu's VIX percentile gate

**This is the only place in the project where two pre-registered criteria give opposite answers on
the same component.** It is also the only teammate component whose owner actually ported onto the
shared base — so the component being dropped belongs to the one person who did the integration work
properly. That is uncomfortable, and it is not a reason to decide it differently.

### What the gate is

Halve exposure when VIX sits in the top fifth of its trailing three years
(`exposure_scalar(VIX, lookback=756, q=0.80, low_mult=0.5)`). One line, no fitting, no parameters
beyond the two stated. It reconstructs **exactly** — no file needed, just `data/raw` — and reproduces
Dafu's committed headline to five decimal places.

### The two answers

**The tail objective says ACCEPT** (`evidence/p4_reverdict_tail_objective.csv`). Standalone on the
baseline it costs **−0.0007** net Sharpe and buys **+4.82 pp** of MaxDD. Against a budget of 0.02
Sharpe for ≥ 1.0 pp of drawdown, that is not a marginal pass — it is one of five rules out of twelve
that flip from reject to accept once the objective is the one the desk actually stated.

**The slot rule says REJECT** (`evidence/p4_combined_ladder.csv`). It is the only component in the
file with `slot_earned=False` on the add pass, and the only one flagged `ladders_agree=False`.

### Measured four times, on four different stacks

| Pass | Measured on | Windows | `slot_earned` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `add` rung 2 | added to `duration` | 3 / 6 | **False** |
| `final` rung 2 | added to `duration` *(same two books)* | 3 / 6 | **False** |
| `loo` | removed from `duration\|regime\|skew` | 3 / 6 | **False** |
| `final_loo` | removed from `duration\|skew` — **the stack actually proposed** | **4 / 6** | **True** |

Three reject, one accepts. And the one that accepts is the one guardrail §6.13 privileges: *a
component's slot verdict must be re-measured on the stack actually proposed.* So this cannot be
waved away as one odd measurement against three good ones.

### Two things had to be diagnosed before the verdict could be reached

**The `ladders_agree=False` flag is an artifact.** `combined_engine.py:670-672` compares the add
pass's `slot_earned` (windows **and** cost) against the loo pass's `d_net_sharpe > 0` (Sharpe sign
only). Those are different tests. Read like-for-like — `slot_earned` against `slot_earned` — the two
ladders **agree, both False**, and the VIX gate is the only component where the two conventions
diverge, which is exactly why it is the only row flagged. The published "the two ladders disagree,
therefore the component is not robust" framing rests on that mismatch. **Correcting it makes the
rejection stronger, not weaker** — but it also means the rejection cannot be justified by *this*
argument, which is why the verdict below rests on something else.

**The identical −0.190665 MaxDD is not a bug.** The two books report the same maximum drawdown to
six decimal places, which looks like a copy-paste error and is not. `run("COMBINED")`'s drawdown
episode is **2013-05-17 → 2013-12-05** (the taper tantrum), and the gate is fully invested on **all
145 days of it** — `(gate < 1)` is 0.000 for 2012, 2013 *and* 2014. The daily returns over the entire
peak-to-trough stretch are identical, so the drawdown depth must be too. The residual 1.6e-15 gap is
floating-point accumulation in a wealth curve that runs from 2007, and the committed CSV shows the
same 1.58e-15.

The consequence matters more than the diagnosis: **whole-sample MaxDD is structurally blind to this
gate.** Any argument for or against it that leans on MaxDD is leaning on a statistic that cannot see
it. CVaR₉₉ is the tail measure that moves: 0.020025 → 0.018942, −5.4% relative.

### The decision

**REJECT — the gate stays out of `COMBINED`.**

The two criteria are not in conflict because one is right and the other wrong. They answer different
questions. The **tail objective** is an admission test: is this rule worth considering at all, given
the desk cares about capital preservation rather than Sharpe? Yes. The **slot rule** is a membership
test: has it earned a place in *this* stack, alongside components competing for the same risk
budget? It was written later, specifically to decide what goes into the combined book, and it is
the more demanding of the two. Where a component clears the admission test and fails the membership
test, it is a candidate that did not make the team.

Within the slot rule, criterion (i) is an **add-one-in** measurement by construction — that is the
orientation `_slot_verdict(res, prev)` uses on the `add` and `final` ladders, and it is what
"improves ... in ≥4 of 6 windows" means when a component is being added. On both stacks where the
gate is added, it improves 3 of 6. Criterion (iii), leave-one-out, is a separate conjunct, and it
passes. **A conjunction with a failing term fails.**

The honest counter-argument, stated at full strength because it deserves to be: §6.13 says the
verdict must be re-measured on the stack actually proposed, `final_loo` is that measurement, and it
returns 4/6 and `slot_earned=True`. Someone applying §6.13 as the senior rule reaches the opposite
conclusion. **What decides it is not which reading is cleverer but when each was written.** The slot
rule and its add-one-in orientation were fixed before any component was measured. §6.13 was added
mid-execution, to fix a real defect — measuring survivors against a stack containing a
since-rejected component — and that defect applies to the `loo` row, not to the add rows. Extending
it to overturn a pre-registered add-pass, *after* seeing that doing so is worth +0.043 Sharpe, is
exactly the move pre-registration exists to prevent.

### What the decision costs, stated rather than buried

| | Net Sharpe | MaxDD | CVaR₉₉ | Turnover |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `run("COMBINED")` — **shipped** | 0.4891 | −19.07% | 0.0200 | 0.5902 |
| `run("COMBINED_TAIL")` — the gate included | **0.5323** | −19.07% | **0.0189** | 0.6051 |
| **Cost of rejecting** | **−0.043** | 0.00 (structurally) | **+5.4% rel.** | −0.015 |

**0.043 of net Sharpe and 5.4% of relative CVaR₉₉ is what pre-registration costs here.** Paying it
is the point. A rule that only binds when it is free is not a rule.

So the alternative ships as a runnable book rather than a paragraph:

```python
run("COMBINED_TAIL")     # gross 0.6808 · net 0.5323 · MaxDD -19.07% · CVaR99 0.0189
```

It is **not the strategy** and is not the default anywhere. It exists so a desk can price the
decision instead of taking it on trust, and so that if someone later concludes §6.13 should have
governed, the book they want is already built, already tested
(`tests/test_combined.py::test_combined_tail_is_the_documented_alternative`) and already reconciled
to a committed ladder rung that has been in the evidence since Phase 4.

## D2 — the one positive result, and why it is not in the book

The FX volatility risk premium is the only non-null this project produced. `short_vol` scores
Sharpe **1.27**, the cross-sectional `vrp_xs` book **1.69**. Neither is in the strategy, and the
reason is not caution.

- **Monthly**, not daily. **21-name** option universe, not 27. **Gross of option bid/ask** — there is
  no bid/ask on any volatility surface in `data/raw`, so the premium is unpriced by construction.
- Read off `evidence/p3_d2_breakeven_cost.csv`'s own `beats_both_bars` column, the widest vol spread
  at which each book still clears both bars is **0.25 / 0.25 / 0.10** vol points. Interbank G10 1M
  ATM trades inside roughly **0.2**. So the headline `vrp_xs` book **dies inside G10 interbank** —
  and the EM names, where the spread is widest, are precisely its standing shorts.

It has a verdict row, its own report chapter (`report/08_volatility_risk_premium.md`), and a
`basis="monthly_uncosted"` label everywhere it appears in `evidence/final_comparison.csv`.
`reproduce.py` never prints it beside a costed daily Sharpe. **A 1.69 Sharpe sitting anywhere near
the tradable book would be the most misleading number in the package**, which is why it is stated
loudly and excluded explicitly rather than quietly dropped.
